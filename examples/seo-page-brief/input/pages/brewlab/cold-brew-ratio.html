Cold brew ratio guide: concentrate vs ready-to-drink

By Sam Ortega · Updated August 19, 2026

The cold brew ratio is the weight of coffee compared with the weight of water. It is the single biggest factor in how strong your cold brew tastes, more than steeping time or grind size. There are two families of ratios, and mixing them up is the most common reason people end up with cold brew that is either sludgy or watery.

Concentrate or ready-to-drink?

A concentrate is brewed strong, at roughly 1:4 to 1:5, and diluted before drinking, usually with an equal amount of water or milk. Ready-to-drink cold brew is brewed at 1:8 to 1:12 and poured straight over ice. Concentrate keeps longer and is more flexible; ready-to-drink is simpler and needs less coffee per glass once you account for dilution.

Converting grams to cups and scoops

Weighing is far more reliable, but if you only have measuring cups: one cup of coarsely ground coffee weighs roughly 80 to 90 grams, and one cup of water weighs about 237 grams. For a 1:5 concentrate, use about one cup of ground coffee for every four cups of water. A standard coffee scoop holds around 10 grams of coarse grounds.

Why volume measurements drift

Dark roasts are less dense than light roasts, and coarse grounds take up more space than fine ones. A cup of dark-roast coarse grounds can weigh 20 percent less than a cup of light-roast grounds, which changes your ratio without you noticing.

How steeping time changes the ratio you need

Longer steeps extract more, so a weaker ratio steeped for 20 hours can taste as strong as a stronger ratio steeped for 12. As a starting point, steep 12 to 18 hours at room temperature or 18 to 24 hours in the fridge, then adjust the ratio before you adjust the time.

Adjusting for taste

If your cold brew tastes weak, move one step stronger on the chart. If it tastes bitter or muddy, use a coarser grind or shorten the steep before changing the ratio. Change one variable at a time and write down what you did.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best cold brew ratio?

For most people, 1:5 for concentrate or 1:10 for ready-to-drink.

How many scoops of coffee for cold brew?

About 1 cup of ground coffee per 4 cups of water for concentrate, or roughly 10 scoops per litre.

Can I reuse the grounds?

No. Almost all of the flavour is extracted in the first brew; a second brew tastes flat and woody.
