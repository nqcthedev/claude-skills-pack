# SEO brief: cold brew ratio

| | |
|---|---|
| Page | **New page** `/blog/cold-brew-ratio`, linked from the existing guide `/blog/cold-brew` (see "Why a new page") |
| Search intent | Informational - the searcher wants a number (how much coffee per water) and enough context to choose between concentrate and ready-to-drink |
| Audience | Home brewers making cold brew in a jar or pitcher; many own a scale, some only measuring cups |
| Goal / conversion | Click through to the shop's cold brew coffees; secondary: subscription sign-up |
| Target length | 700-900 words plus 2 tables (competitors: 63-500 words, median 288) |
| Data source | Route A - web-insights MCP (`fetch_page`, `extract_structured`, `compare_pages`, `seo_audit`), 12 tool calls, retrieved 20 Nov 2026. Raw results in `research/`. |

## Recommended metadata

- **Title** (49 chars): Cold Brew Ratio Chart for Every Jar Size (Tested)
  - Alternative (54 chars): Cold Brew Ratio: Concentrate vs Ready-to-Drink (Chart)
- **Meta description** (148 chars): Cold brew ratio chart for concentrate and ready-to-drink, scaled to your jar, plus what each ratio tastes like from our roasters' side-by-side test.
- **H1**: Cold brew ratio: how much coffee for concentrate and ready-to-drink
- **Slug**: `/blog/cold-brew-ratio`

## Why a new page

All three ranking pages are dedicated to the ratio: each title starts with "Cold Brew Ratio" or tests ratios, and each puts the ratio chart near the top. Our current guide targets "how to make cold brew" and covers the ratio in one section ("Steeping time and ratio"). Stretching that guide to compete would weaken it for its own query. A focused ratio page, linked both ways with the guide, matches the intent of both queries.

**Fix the existing guide too.** It recommends "a ratio of 1:8 to make a concentrate" and then dilutes it 1:1. Every competitor calls 1:8 a *ready-to-drink* strength (BrewLab: concentrate 1:4-1:5; Bean & Barrel: "If you drink it black over ice, brew 1:8 and skip the dilution step"; The Daily Grind: "Regular: 1 cup coffee to 8 cups water"). Diluting a 1:8 brew gives roughly 1:16 in the glass - weaker than anything the ranking pages recommend. Align the guide with the house recommendation that comes out of the test below.

## Why this page can win

- **Original test data from roasters.** Only Bean & Barrel tested ratios, with one medium-roast bean and four tasters, and it lists roast level as untested ("whether a light roast needs a stronger ratio"). Lumen can test two roasts and report it - the strongest experience signal on this results page.
- **Ratios scaled to real containers.** BrewLab's chart uses 500 g and 1,000 g of water only; nobody tells you what to put in a 1 L jar, a 2 L pitcher or a single glass.
- **Both measurement systems in one table.** BrewLab converts to cups in prose; The Daily Grind gives cups only. A grams + cups + scoops table answers both audiences.
- **Troubleshooting by taste.** BrewLab's "Adjusting for taste" is three sentences; the others have none.

## Outline

### H2: The short answer: our cold brew ratio chart

- Open with the two numbers most people need: concentrate ratio and ready-to-drink ratio, as recommended by the Lumen test (fill in after the test; the SERP consensus is 1:4-1:5 for concentrate and 1:8-1:12 ready-to-drink).
- Table: style · ratio · coffee · water · how to serve, for 1:4, 1:5, 1:8, 1:10, 1:12 - consensus topic (3/3 competitors give ratio numbers).
- One sentence on what a ratio means: grams of coffee to grams of water.

### H2: Concentrate or ready-to-drink?

- When to brew each: milk drinks and longer storage (concentrate) vs black over ice with no dilution step (ready-to-drink) - consensus (3/3).
- How to dilute concentrate (1:1 with water or milk as the default) - consensus (2/3).

### H2: How much coffee for your jar

- **Gap #2.** Table rows: 1 glass (250 ml), 500 ml bottle, 1 L jar, 2 L pitcher; columns: coffee in grams for concentrate and for ready-to-drink.
- Note that a 1 L jar holds less than 1 L of water once the coffee is in it; leave about 10% headroom.

### H2: No scale? Cups and scoops

- **Gap #3.** Grams-to-cups-and-scoops table for the same ratios, using the coffee you sell (weigh a level cup of your coarse grind; do not copy competitors' numbers - BrewLab gives 80-90 g per cup and 10 g per scoop).
- Why volume drifts with roast and grind (BrewLab covers this in an H3; keep it to two sentences).

### H2: We tested 5 ratios with a light and a dark roast

- **Gap #1, the centre of the page.** Same method as below for each roast; report what each ratio tasted like and which one the team preferred, in a table.
- Method to publish: water weight, grind setting, steep time and temperature, dilution, number of tasters, blind or not. Photos of the jars with descriptive alt text.
- State the house recommendation that comes out of it, and use it in the first section and in the existing guide.

### H2: Steeping time and the ratio work together

- Consensus (3/3): steep ranges at room temperature vs fridge, and why a longer steep can stand in for a stronger ratio.
- Tip: change the ratio before you change the time.

### H2: Too weak, too strong, bitter or sour? Fix it

- **Gap #4.** Four-row table: symptom → most likely cause → change one thing.

### H2: Frequently asked questions

Answer each in 1-2 sentences:

- **What is the best cold brew ratio?** The test's recommendation for concentrate and for ready-to-drink.
- **How many scoops of coffee for cold brew?** From the cups-and-scoops table.
- **Can I reuse the grounds?** No - almost all the flavour comes out in the first brew.
- **How long does cold brew keep?** Concentrate up to two weeks in the fridge, diluted cold brew two to three days (our guide already says this; keep both pages consistent).
- **Is cold brew stronger than regular coffee?** The concentrate is; diluted, a glass is similar to a cup of filter coffee. Only add caffeine figures with a cited source.

### Closing CTA

Two coffees suited to cold brew from the shop, with one line each on why, and a link to the subscription.

## Schema markup

- **Article** with author (the roaster who ran the test) and `dateModified` - BrewLab is the only competitor showing an author and update date.
- **FAQPage** for the visible FAQ is fine for search engines' understanding, but do not expect FAQ rich results: Google has limited them to a small set of authoritative sites since 2023. Likewise, skip HowTo markup for rich results (deprecated by Google in 2023), even though BrewLab uses it.

## Technical notes

From `seo_audit` of the current guide (score 94/100): the only issues were *served over HTTP* (an artefact of the local test server used for this research - the live site uses HTTPS) and *no `twitter:card` tag*. Add `<meta name="twitter:card" content="summary_large_image">` to the blog template so both pages get large-image cards.
