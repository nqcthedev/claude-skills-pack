## Voice

- **Plain English, confident, specific.** Write like a senior consultant talking to a busy founder. Prefer "We will connect your booking calendar" over "Seamless integration capabilities will be leveraged".
- **"You" and "we".** Address the client directly. Never refer to them as "the client" inside the proposal.
- **Short.** Paragraphs of 1-3 sentences. Bullets for lists of three or more. Aim for a document that prints on 3-5 pages.
- **Outcomes before activities.** Lead each phase with what the client will have at the end, then the work.
- **Honest about uncertainty.** Say "we assume" and "we will confirm in discovery" rather than hiding risk.
- **No hype.** Avoid: revolutionary, cutting-edge, seamless, best-in-class, world-class, synergy, leverage (as a verb), game-changer, robust (unless about engineering), "AI-powered" as decoration.
- **Numbers.** Use numerals for all quantities and money ($4,800, 3 weeks). Show money without decimals unless cents matter.

## Formatting rules

- Title: `Proposal: <outcome> for <Client>` - for example "Proposal: 24/7 appointment booking assistant for BrightSmile Dental".
- Sentence case for headings.
- Pricing always in a table with columns: Item, Effort, Price. Totals in bold. One currency per document.
- Timeline as a table: Week(s), Milestone, What we need from you.
- Dates in the format 12 Jan 2027 when dates are used.
- End with a clear next step and who does what, never with "let us know if you have questions".

## Words we use

| Prefer | Instead of |
|---|---|
| launch, go live | deploy to production (in client-facing text) |
| assistant, chatbot | AI agent (unless the client used that term) |
| we will confirm | TBD |
| fixed fee | flat rate |
| change request | scope creep |
